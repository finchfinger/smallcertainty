### Best Chef’s Knife

**MAC Professional MTH-80**

The MAC Professional MTH-80 is the knife we would put in nearly every serious home kitchen. It combines the thin, decisive cutting of a Japanese blade with the familiar length and handling of a Western chef’s knife. At 8.5 inches, it is large enough for a cabbage, manageable around garlic, and noticeably less tiring than the thick German knives that once defined the category.

Its stamped high-carbon stainless blade is ground to a fine edge and tempered hard enough to hold it, without becoming precious laboratory equipment. The shallow dimples help wet slices release, although technique matters more than the feature. A full tang, three rivets, and a shaped pakkawood handle give the knife reassuring balance. The slightly raised handle also leaves useful clearance for the knuckles.

The MTH-80 rewards a cutting board, regular honing, and occasional professional sharpening. It should be washed by hand, dried promptly, and kept away from bones, frozen food, and dishwasher racks. In return, it passes cleanly through herbs without bruising them, makes onion work calmer, and handles meat and fish with enough finesse that a separate collection of specialist blades becomes optional.

There are harder steels, more elaborate handles, and knives with stronger claims to craft. The MAC wins because its virtues remain apparent on an ordinary Tuesday. It is light but not insubstantial, exact but not temperamental, and adaptable without feeling generic. One good knife should make the rest of the drawer seem unnecessary. This is that knife, provided its owner agrees to keep it sharp.
