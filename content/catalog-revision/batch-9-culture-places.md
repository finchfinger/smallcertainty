# Batch 9 draft

## CULTURE

### Best Magazine

**BRUTUS**

Each issue treats a subject as a world to enter rather than a content category to service. Since 1980, the Japanese title has moved through food, cities, music, architecture, dogs, and domestic life with equal seriousness. Its curiosity is broad but never vague, and the physical magazine makes editorial judgment visible before a word is translated.

Published by Magazine House, BRUTUS builds thematic issues through commissioned photography, illustration, maps, interviews, lists, and dense typographic sequences. Covers change character according to the subject rather than obeying a rigid template, while the interiors sustain rhythm across short notices and long features. Japanese text may limit some readers, but captions, images, and information design communicate a remarkable amount through structure alone.

Issues can be difficult and expensive to find outside Japan, and their paper abundance is not easily reconciled with casual consumption. Buy subjects worth keeping, use specialist bookshops, and resist treating the archive as decorative wallpaper. Translation tools help with specific pages, though the magazine rewards slower visual reading. Its breadth means not every issue will matter equally, which is evidence of a publication taking real editorial risks.

Many magazines preserve a house style by repeating the same five subjects and voices. BRUTUS preserves an attitude instead: alert, informed, worldly, and willing to make an entire issue about one good question. It proves that service journalism can be beautiful without becoming empty luxury. Start with a subject already loved, keep the issues that alter how it is seen, and let the rest circulate.

[Visit site](https://magazineworld.jp/brutus/)

Honorable mentions: The World of Interiors; Apartamento

### Best Newspaper

**Financial Times**

The Financial Times connects money, politics, business, culture, and power with unusual international range. Its salmon paper is memorable, but the distinction is editorial: reporting assumes that events cross borders and that companies matter beyond their products. The daily edition provides hierarchy to a noisy world, while the weekend paper makes room for books, travel, food, and private life.

Correspondents report from a genuinely global network, supported by specialist desks that understand markets, policy, industry, and institutions. The print paper uses compact typography, clear sectioning, restrained graphics, and data visualization that clarifies rather than decorates. Digital subscriptions add archives, alerts, newsletters, and the Lex column. Opinion is labeled, news remains reported, and corrections are treated as part of the publication rather than an embarrassment.

The subscription is expensive, the financial vocabulary assumes attention, and its institutional perspective should never be mistaken for complete neutrality. Read it alongside local reporting and publications from outside its commercial frame. The app can encourage endless checking, so a daily paper or scheduled briefing often provides the better boundary. Weekend supplements accumulate quickly; retain the pieces worth returning to and recycle the rest.

A newspaper cannot remove uncertainty, but it can make relationships legible and provide a common record against which claims are tested. The FT does this with rigor, economy, and a tone that rarely confuses volume with authority. It is especially useful for readers whose work is not finance, because capital quietly organizes so much else. Subscribe in the format actually read, and make regular attention more valuable than constant updates.

[Visit site](https://www.ft.com/)

Honorable mentions: Neue Zürcher Zeitung; Le Monde

### Best Cookbook

**Essentials of Classic Italian Cooking**

Marcella Hazan’s Essentials of Classic Italian Cooking teaches judgment rather than merely supplying dinners. Its recipes are direct, its regional distinctions matter, and its instructions explain what heat, fat, time, and sequence do to ingredients. The book has no interest in novelty plating or culinary autobiography. It wants the reader to understand why a sauce becomes itself.

The volume combines Hazan’s two foundational books into a broad course covering pasta, risotto, soups, vegetables, fish, meat, breads, and desserts. Recipes such as tomato sauce with onion and butter demonstrate how few ingredients can produce depth when proportion and cooking are exact. Headnotes provide cultural and technical context, while the index and chapter structure make the heavy book function as a working reference.

The prose can be firm, ingredient assumptions sometimes reflect an earlier American market, and photographs are absent. Those qualities force attention onto method and result. Read a recipe completely, use the specified pan when its shape matters, and resist substitutions during the first attempt. The binding benefits from a stand and clean hands, though stains eventually become a more useful record than pristine pages.

Cookbooks often promise ease by concealing the decisions that make food good. Hazan respects the reader enough to name them, then repeats principles until instinct begins to form. The book can guide a first tomato sauce and still settle an argument after decades of cooking. Buy one durable copy, cook across chapters rather than collecting favorites, and allow technique to reduce the need for further Italian cookbooks.

[Visit site](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/77501/essentials-of-classic-italian-cooking-by-marcella-hazan/)

Honorable mentions: Mastering the Art of French Cooking; The River Cafe Cookbook

## PLACES

### Best Hotel

**Ett Hem, Stockholm**

Ett Hem behaves like an exceptionally well-run house rather than a procession of branded hospitality gestures. Set in a 1910 Arts and Crafts townhouse in Stockholm, it offers rooms, libraries, sitting spaces, a garden, and a kitchen guests are encouraged to inhabit. Service is attentive but rarely theatrical, making comfort feel discovered rather than delivered on schedule.

Designer Ilse Crawford worked with owner Jeanette Mix to preserve the building’s domestic scale while adding contemporary furniture, warm lighting, tactile textiles, and art that rewards repeated looking. Bedrooms vary with the architecture, bathrooms use stone and brass without becoming showrooms, and public rooms flow around fireplaces and tables. Later additions expand capacity while maintaining the sense that each object belongs to a particular room.

The intimacy comes at a high price, and travelers who prefer a full-service lobby, large gym, or strict anonymity may find the house format intrusive. Rooms differ enough that preferences should be discussed before booking. Breakfast and dinner are worth taking in, while the honor-bar approach assumes considerate guests. Its quiet Östermalm location rewards walking, though central sights are not all immediately outside the door.

Hotels often pursue consistency until place disappears. Ett Hem offers a stronger form of reliability: thoughtful food, excellent beds, perceptive staff, and rooms whose differences are part of their character. It understands luxury as freedom from friction rather than an accumulation of amenities. Stay long enough to use the library and garden, eat at the shared table at least once, and let Stockholm begin from a convincing home.

[Visit site](https://www.etthem.se/)

Honorable mentions: Hotel Il Pellicano, Porto Ercole; Aman Tokyo

### Best Restaurant

**Asador Etxebarri, Atxondo**

Chef Victor Arguinzoniz uses fire not as theater but as a precise cooking medium. In a Basque village beneath Mount Anboto, the kitchen applies different woods, embers, heights, and timings to vegetables, seafood, meat, and even milk. The meal is technically extraordinary, yet ingredients remain recognizable and the room never needs to imitate spectacle.

Arguinzoniz designed adjustable grills and tools that allow delicate items to encounter smoke and heat without being consumed by them. Local produce, house-made chorizo, prawns, fish, and aged beef arrive in a sequence shaped by season and supply. The dining room occupies a restrained stone building overlooking the valley, while service explains enough to orient the guest and lets aroma, texture, and temperature complete the account.

Reservations are difficult, the tasting menu is long and expensive, and reaching Atxondo requires planning. This is not casual village lunch despite its setting. Arrive on time, disclose dietary restrictions early, and avoid building the experience around ranking or photography. Smoke is used with greater subtlety than the restaurant’s reputation suggests; guests expecting every plate to announce the grill may miss the more interesting calibrations.

Restaurants endure when method serves appetite and place rather than becoming a performance detached from both. Etxebarri joins exceptional raw materials, custom engineering, and accumulated knowledge of fire into food that could exist nowhere else. The recommendation does not mean every journey requires a pilgrimage, but for one meal demonstrating what specialization can achieve without excess, this is where the search can end.

[Visit site](https://asadoretxebarri.com/)

Honorable mentions: Elkano, Getaria; Le Bernardin, New York

### Best Bar

**Bar Benfiddich, Tokyo**

Hiroyasu Kayama approaches drinks as a conversation between agriculture, history, and immediate improvisation. Hidden in a Tokyo office building, the room is dark, compact, and centered on a working bar crowded with bottles, herbs, tools, and ingredients from Kayama’s family farm. There is no conventional menu, but there is close attention to what a guest might enjoy.

Kayama distills, infuses, grinds, and mixes ingredients with techniques drawn from apothecaries as much as contemporary cocktail bars. Absinthe, amaro, tea, fruit, roots, and spices appear according to season and question. Antique glassware and deliberate handwork slow the exchange without turning it into choreography. Drinks are complex but remain drinkable, and their stories emerge from the process rather than being printed as concepts.

The no-menu format asks for trust and a little conversation. Seating is limited, reservations or current entry procedures should be checked, and the building can be easy to pass. Tell the bartender what flavors or spirits are disliked, then allow some latitude. This is not the place for a large group, fast round, or loud celebration. The intimacy that creates the experience also establishes its necessary limits.

Cocktail culture often repeats historical recipes or presents laboratory technique as entertainment. Benfiddich uses both knowledge and invention to make something personal to the room, season, and drinker. It feels singular without becoming self-important, which is rarer than novelty. Go with one companion, pay attention to the preparation, and accept that the best drink may be one that cannot be ordered exactly the same way again.

[Visit site](https://www.instagram.com/barbenfiddich/)

Honorable mentions: Bar Leone, Hong Kong; Connaught Bar, London

### Best Café

**Café de l’Ambre, Tokyo**

Café de l’Ambre has spent decades narrowing its attention to coffee itself. Opened in Ginza in 1948, the small room serves carefully aged beans, dark roasts, and drinks prepared with a patience at odds with the speed surrounding it. The atmosphere is serious but not sterile, and the equipment bears the polish of constant work rather than nostalgic staging.

Beans are selected, stored, roasted, ground, and brewed according to their individual condition. Cloth filters, copper kettles, hand pouring, and precise temperature control produce cups with remarkable clarity and depth despite the dark roasting associated with kissaten. The menu includes straight coffees and composed drinks such as Queen Amber. Wood, smoke-darkened surfaces, and a long counter place preparation at the center of the room.

This is not contemporary light-roast coffee, and visitors seeking acidity, laptops, or elaborate milk drinks should adjust expectations. Seating is limited, service follows the café’s rhythm, and some aged coffees command serious prices. Order one cup before constructing a tasting, watch without obstructing, and allow flavors to cool and change. The room is best experienced quietly, not used as a checklist stop between Ginza purchases.

Great cafés create a public interior around a particular way of spending time. L’Ambre does so through concentration, skilled repetition, and the conviction that coffee can support an institution without constant reinvention. It is historical because the practice has continued, not because the room is preserved as a set. Sit at the counter, choose with guidance, and let one deliberate cup reset the pace of the day.

[Visit site](https://www.instagram.com/cafedelambre/)

Honorable mentions: Café Sperl, Vienna; Koffee Mameya Kakeru, Tokyo

### Best Bakery

**Hart Bageri, Copenhagen**

Hart Bageri joins the precision of modern fermentation with the immediate pleasure of a neighborhood shop. Founded by baker Richard Hart in Copenhagen, it makes bread with character, pastries with disciplined richness, and savory food worth arriving for before lunch. The shops feel contemporary without turning flour sacks and stainless steel into a lifestyle display.

Sourdough loaves develop deep crust, open but useful crumb, and enough acidity to remain lively with butter, cheese, or soup. Laminated pastries show careful layering without becoming brittle sculpture, while cardamom buns, cookies, and changing specials make room for appetite alongside technique. Milling, grain selection, fermentation, and high heat are treated as linked decisions rather than talking points attached after baking.

Popular locations produce queues, favorite pastries sell out, and quality bread is best eaten rather than carried around Copenhagen for photographs. Arrive early for selection, but return later if the line overwhelms the pleasure. Loaves keep well cut-side down and freeze cleanly in slices. The richness of the pastry case rewards choosing one or two things, especially because the bread deserves equal attention and travels better.

The renewed interest in artisan baking has produced many technically excellent loaves and far too much reverence around breakfast. Hart retains seriousness while remembering generosity, speed, and the democratic function of a bakery. Its best work is precise without becoming austere and indulgent without becoming novelty. Buy a loaf for later, eat something warm nearby, and judge the place by what remains compelling after the first bite.

[Visit site](https://hartbageri.com/)

Honorable mentions: Poilâne, Paris; Sullivan Street Bakery, New York

### Best Bookshop

**Daunt Books Marylebone, London**

Daunt Books Marylebone’s celebrated Edwardian gallery supports, rather than substitutes for, intelligent bookselling. Daylight enters through the long skylight, oak balconies rise above the central floor, and travel books are arranged by country alongside history, fiction, memoir, and politics. The organization allows a destination to become a subject rather than a shelf of guidebooks.

Opened as an antiquarian bookseller and completed in 1912, the building uses long sight lines, restrained timber shelving, and a mezzanine reached by narrow stairs. Daunt’s current selection combines new literary fiction, nonfiction, children’s books, travel writing, and editions chosen with clear taste. Tables are edited enough to guide discovery without becoming piles of whatever publishing is currently promoting most heavily.

Marylebone can be crowded, the aisles narrow, and the beautiful room attracts visitors with little intention of buying a book. Go early, ask the staff for a recommendation, and move beyond the central gallery into the quieter front rooms. Prices are standard retail, while the cloth tote has become almost too visible. Buy it only if a bag is actually needed; the book remains the better souvenir.

Online retail makes every title available and almost none of them discoverable. Daunt restores discovery through architecture, classification, and booksellers who place works in useful company. It is a destination that still functions as a neighborhood shop, which keeps admiration from becoming museum behavior. Arrive with one question rather than a list, leave with a book not previously known, and let browsing justify the journey.

[Visit site](https://dauntbooks.co.uk/shops/marylebone/)

Honorable mentions: Tsutaya Books Daikanyama, Tokyo; Libreria Bocca, Milan

### Best Record Store

**Disk Union, Tokyo**

Disk Union treats recorded music as several overlapping cultures rather than one retail category. Across its Tokyo branches, entire buildings and floors specialize in jazz, classical, rock, punk, soul, electronic music, and Japanese releases. The density can be intimidating, but the classification is exact enough that browsing becomes education rather than simply searching for an item already known.

New vinyl, used pressings, CDs, box sets, books, and equipment sit within tightly labeled sections divided by genre, artist, format, country, and condition. Handwritten notes and specialist selections reveal the staff behind the system. Used records are graded carefully, rare objects receive context, and ordinary affordable releases remain abundant. Different branches preserve distinct identities rather than duplicating one central inventory across convenient locations.

The stores require time, and international collectors should understand grading, tax, luggage weight, and regional formats before enthusiasm takes over. Begin with the branch devoted to the music actually loved, use want lists lightly, and ask staff when classification defeats navigation. Listening stations and current procedures vary. A record is not improved by rarity alone, so condition and the likelihood of repeated play should govern the purchase.

Streaming has made access nearly universal while making musical context strangely thin. Disk Union restores context through adjacency, expertise, and the physical evidence of how scenes and labels developed. It is valuable even when nothing is purchased, though leaving empty-handed requires discipline. Choose one branch, browse beyond the expected alphabet, and let a well-selected record carry some of Tokyo’s extraordinary listening culture home.

[Visit site](https://diskunion.net/)

Honorable mentions: Rough Trade East, London; Amoeba Music, Los Angeles

### Best Department Store

**Isetan Shinjuku, Tokyo**

Isetan Shinjuku still makes the breadth of retail feel like a civic pleasure rather than an exhausted real-estate format. Fashion, food, beauty, homewares, and craft are edited with unusual confidence, while each floor retains enough identity to reward wandering. The main building is large but legible, and service is precise without turning every encounter into luxury theater.

Departments mix international houses with Japanese designers, seasonal exhibitions, pop-ups, and specialist concessions selected to create useful contrast. Display changes constantly, but circulation, lighting, typography, and staff knowledge keep the experience coherent. The basement food halls are a city in miniature, moving from produce and prepared dishes to sweets, tea, wine, and immaculate gifts. Packaging becomes part of service rather than an afterthought.

The store is crowded at peak hours, the basement can overwhelm hunger with choice, and tax-free procedures require time. Arrive near opening, identify one or two floors, and resist treating every limited collaboration as discovery. Sizes and stock may be specific to Japan, while international shipping varies by department. Eat something before buying food gifts, and carry fewer packages than the staff are capable of beautifully preparing.

Department stores fail when scale replaces judgment. Isetan succeeds because curation, hospitality, and constant renewal give scale a reason to exist. It can introduce a young designer, supply an ordinary kitchen tool, fit a suit, and assemble dinner beneath one roof without flattening those acts into sameness. Start at the top or basement, move slowly, and let comparison become part of the city’s cultural life.

[Visit site](https://www.mistore.jp/store/shinjuku.html)

Honorable mentions: Mitsukoshi Nihombashi, Tokyo; KaDeWe, Berlin

### Best Cinema

**Pathé Tuschinski, Amsterdam**

Pathé Tuschinski makes watching a film feel public and ceremonial without allowing architecture to overpower the screen. Opened in 1921 by Abraham Tuschinski, the building combines Art Deco, Amsterdam School, and theatrical fantasy in a sequence of foyers, stairs, murals, lamps, and carpets. The main auditorium restores anticipation before the lights fall.

Designed by Hijman Louis de Jong with contributions from numerous artists and craftspeople, the cinema layers carved wood, stained glass, glazed tile, painted ornament, and custom lettering. Restoration has preserved the principal interiors while introducing contemporary projection, sound, seating, and accessibility. The building contains several screens, but Grote Zaal provides the full architectural experience, including its stage, balcony, organ, and rich proscenium.

Not every screening uses the main auditorium, so confirm the room before booking rather than assuming the address guarantees it. Commercial programming varies, crowds gather in the foyer, and premium seats are not automatically the best viewing distance. Arrive early enough to look without obstructing circulation. The interior rewards attention, but phones and photographs should disappear once the film begins, as they should in every cinema.

Home screens offer convenience and technical quality, but cinema depends on shared scale, darkness, and the decision to give a film uninterrupted time. Tuschinski adds architecture that marks that decision without turning it into nostalgia. It remains a working commercial cinema, not a preserved palace awaiting special events. Choose a film worthy of the room, sit near the center, and let arrival become part of viewing.

[Visit site](https://www.pathe.nl/en/cinemas/pathe-tuschinski)

Honorable mentions: Metrograph, New York; Cinéma du Panthéon, Paris

### Best Market

**Marché d’Aligre, Paris**

Marché d’Aligre combines daily food, neighborhood argument, immigrant commerce, and accidental discovery within a few dense streets of the twelfth arrondissement. Produce stalls fill rue d’Aligre, permanent traders occupy the covered Marché Beauvau, and secondhand sellers spread across place d’Aligre. The result is useful before it is picturesque, which is why it remains worth visiting.

Fruit and vegetables arrive in volume and at varied prices, while fishmongers, butchers, cheese sellers, spice shops, bakeries, cafés, and North African grocers extend the market beyond its official boundaries. The covered hall provides structure and specialist continuity; the outdoor stalls contribute speed, season, and competitive noise. Flea-market tables add tools, records, crockery, clothes, and objects whose value depends on looking rather than branding.

Go in the morning, bring cash and a bag, watch local customers before blocking a stall, and accept that the best produce changes by day and hour. Sunday is lively and crowded, Monday quiet because much is closed. Not every antique is treasure, nor every bargain worth carrying home. Buy food to eat nearby, greet vendors, and remember that photography is secondary to people completing ordinary work.

Famous markets often become stages where tourism observes a version of commerce no longer needed by residents. Aligre resists because its prices, range, and surrounding shops still serve the neighborhood. It offers no single perfect purchase, but it makes choosing dinner a form of urban attention. Walk the outdoor line, enter Beauvau, stop for coffee, and let appetite organize the visit.

[Visit site](https://www.paris.fr/lieux/marche-d-aligre-5461)

Honorable mentions: Nishiki Market, Kyoto; Borough Market, London

### Best Museum

**Louisiana Museum of Modern Art, Humlebæk**

Art, architecture, landscape, and the movement between them remain inseparable. Set above the Øresund north of Copenhagen, the museum extends through low brick-and-timber wings, sculpture gardens, courtyards, and changing views of water. Its scale supports major exhibitions without turning the visitor into circulation data, and the intervals between galleries make attention renewable.

Architects Vilhelm Wohlert and Jørgen Bo developed the museum incrementally from 1958, connecting an existing villa through glazed corridors and human-scaled rooms. The collection emphasizes postwar and contemporary art, including sculpture placed in direct relation to land and sky. Temporary exhibitions, a strong children’s wing, concert hall, library, café, and design shop broaden use while preserving the primacy of looking.

Louisiana becomes crowded on weekends and during major shows, while the beautiful setting can tempt visitors to treat art as scenery. Arrive early or late, allow time for the south and north wings, and walk the grounds in weather rather than waiting for ideal sun. Public transport from Copenhagen is straightforward, followed by a short walk. Eat at the café only if doing so does not compress the galleries.

The best museums do more than hold good objects. They establish conditions in which scale, sequence, surprise, and rest sharpen perception. Louisiana has expanded without losing those conditions, a rare institutional achievement. It deserves a half day rather than a hurried excursion, and repeated visits change with season as much as exhibition. Begin without a checklist, follow the building toward the sea, and let art share space with life.

[Visit site](https://louisiana.dk/en/)

Honorable mentions: Fondation Beyeler, Riehen; The Menil Collection, Houston

### Best Public Library

**Oodi, Helsinki**

Oodi expands the idea of free civic space without weakening the central place of books. Opposite Finland’s parliament, the building contains reading rooms, children’s areas, studios, workshops, meeting spaces, games, kitchens, and a cinema beneath one sweeping roof. People may study, make, meet, repair, print, or simply remain indoors without being required to buy anything.

ALA Architects organized the building into three distinct levels. An active ground floor continues the city through entrances, events, and services; a middle floor contains enclosed rooms and tools; the upper Book Heaven opens beneath an undulating white ceiling with trees, shelves, and broad views. A timber-clad bridge spans much of the ground level, allowing large public rooms with surprisingly few columns.

Its popularity creates noise, busy elevators, and competition for specialized rooms, which should be booked when possible. Visitors should remember that Oodi is a working library, not an architectural attraction arranged for photographs. Explore beyond the top floor, return equipment correctly, and respect quiet zones. The café is convenient, but the freedom to occupy space without consumption is the building’s more important hospitality.

Public architecture often promises community through an impressive foyer and little else. Oodi makes the promise operational through staff, equipment, varied rooms, long hours, and a design that allows different kinds of public life to coexist. It is generous without becoming vague and monumental without intimidating use. Enter from the city, borrow or make something, and judge civic ambition by how easily an ordinary afternoon belongs there.

[Visit site](https://oodihelsinki.fi/en/)

Honorable mentions: Stockholm Public Library; NYPL Schwarzman Building

### Best City Park

**Central Park, New York**

Central Park does not provide escape from New York so much as another, necessary version of it. Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux composed meadows, woods, water, paths, bridges, and drives into an artificial landscape whose variety feels inexhaustible. Millions share it without experiencing the same park, and each season reorganizes how the city meets there.

The 1858 Greensward Plan separated transverse traffic and modes of movement while using topography, planting, stonework, and carefully controlled vistas to conceal much of the surrounding grid. The Mall leads ceremonially, the Ramble confuses productively, the Reservoir opens the horizon, and Sheep Meadow supplies democratic emptiness. Playgrounds, ball fields, zoo, theater, memorials, and quieter northern landscapes support uses far beyond scenic walking.

The park is enormous, crowded at famous points, and not uniformly comfortable after dark. Choose one area rather than attempting every landmark, walk or cycle with awareness of designated paths, and check current conditions and closures. Weekday mornings reveal its working rhythms better than a packed summer afternoon. Food, water, weather, and distance still matter despite the surrounding city, especially north of the Reservoir.

Urban parks are sometimes praised as nature preserved, but Central Park is a greater achievement because it is nature deliberately constructed for public use and continually maintained against enormous pressure. Its compromises and history deserve scrutiny, yet its daily generosity remains undeniable. Enter from an unfamiliar corner, leave the itinerary loose, and notice how a designed landscape makes room for solitude, spectacle, exercise, idleness, and everyone else.

[Visit site](https://www.centralparknyc.org/)

Honorable mentions: Englischer Garten, Munich; Yoyogi Park, Tokyo

### Best Beach

**Cala Goloritzé, Sardinia**

Arrival must be earned and the landscape remains stronger than the services usually attached to beauty. A steep path descends through limestone and Mediterranean scrub to a small crescent of pale stones, clear blue water, a natural arch, and the Aguglia pinnacle. There are no beach clubs to mediate the encounter, only shade, water, rock, and time.

The cove was formed by a landslide on Sardinia’s Gulf of Orosei and became a protected natural monument. Springs beneath the sea cool the water and contribute to its startling clarity. Boats must remain offshore, preserving the swimming area and quiet near land. The hiking route begins on the Golgo plateau, dropping substantially through exposed terrain before the harder return ascent.

Access rules, reservations, fees, and daily limits can change, so consult the official Baunei information before setting out. Carry ample water, food, sun protection, proper footwear, and everything back again. Pebbles make a mat useful and water shoes optional. There are no supplies at the cove, phone service may be unreliable, and the climb out punishes a day organized only around the photograph.

The world has many more convenient beaches and several with softer sand. Goloritzé matters because protection, geography, and effort preserve a sense of proportion between visitor and place. Its beauty is not improved by consuming every viewpoint or arriving unprepared. Reserve properly, start early, swim within conditions, and leave before fatigue turns the ascent into resentment. The beach will remain precisely because access retains limits.

[Visit site](https://www.turismobaunei.eu/en/cala-goloritze/)

Honorable mentions: Plage de Saleccia, Corsica; Fteri Beach, Kefalonia

### Best Neighborhood

**Daikanyama, Tokyo**

Daikanyama demonstrates how a large city can sustain quiet streets, independent commerce, ambitious architecture, and ordinary residential life within walking distance of major transport. Low buildings, small gardens, slopes, cafés, fashion shops, studios, and bookshops produce interest without a single monumental attraction. It rewards wandering because the point is the relationship between places rather than one destination.

The neighborhood developed across a varied street pattern between Shibuya, Ebisu, and Nakameguro. Fumihiko Maki’s Hillside Terrace established a model of incremental, mixed-use architecture attentive to pedestrian space, while KDa, Tsutaya Books, and converted houses continue the dialogue at different scales. Retail is edited and often expensive, but side streets retain apartments, schools, clinics, and the infrastructure of daily life.

Weekends bring crowds to the most photographed shops, and the calm can be mistaken for emptiness if the visit is rushed. Begin at Daikanyama Station, walk Hillside Terrace carefully, then drift toward Ebisu or the Meguro River rather than tracing a shopping list. Respect residential lanes, avoid treating private buildings as sets, and stop somewhere long enough to understand the pace that makes the neighborhood coherent.

Good neighborhoods cannot be reduced to a collection of businesses, because those businesses change. Daikanyama’s deeper strength is an urban grain that allows small institutions, thoughtful buildings, greenery, and domestic life to remain in conversation. It feels designed without becoming controlled and fashionable without entirely surrendering to fashion. Visit slowly, look above the storefronts, and carry home a standard for how density can remain humane.

[Visit site](https://www.gotokyo.org/en/destinations/western-tokyo/daikanyama/index.html)

Honorable mentions: Brera, Milan; Frederiksberg, Copenhagen

### Best Train Station

**Zürich Hauptbahnhof**

Zürich Hauptbahnhof converts extraordinary transport complexity into an experience that remains legible, central, and deeply connected to the city. Intercity, international, regional, suburban, tram, and local services converge across surface and underground platforms, yet transfers usually feel like movement through one institution rather than a sequence of terminals. The station works first and impresses through that work.

The monumental 1871 train hall and stone frontage establish civic scale, while later underground levels, passageways, retail streets, and platforms extend capacity beneath the city. Clear signage, synchronized timetables, frequent departures, and visible information allow the architecture to support Swiss rail operations rather than compete with them. The broad main hall hosts markets and events without obstructing its fundamental role as an urban room.

Peak periods are crowded, underground routes take time to learn, and short official connections can feel optimistic to unfamiliar travelers. Check the platform and sector, move before studying a phone, and leave room for people making daily transfers. Shops are convenient but can blur orientation. The station is open and central, so normal city awareness applies, especially late at night and around luggage in busy passages.

Great stations make rail feel not like infrastructure hidden from public life but one of its organizing achievements. Zürich HB joins national precision, international reach, historic architecture, and everyday commerce at the center rather than the edge. It is not flawless, but delays and complexity are communicated within a system built for recovery. Arrive early once, understand the levels, and let later journeys begin without friction.

[Visit site](https://www.sbb.ch/en/travel-information/stations/find-station/zuerich-hb.html)

Honorable mentions: Tokyo Station; Antwerpen-Centraal
